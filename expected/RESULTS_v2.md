# Results of the revised analysis

Generated by scripts/24_summary_v2.py from two recorded runs: the usage analysis (`results/v2/docs/run_manifest.json`)
and the interpretation stages (`results/v2/docs/run_manifest_interpretation.json`). The method is
specified in docs/PLAN_v2.md. Terms: a **window** is the span in which a gene's poly(A) read ends are counted (CDS
5' end to the end of the longest full-length read, or to the next CDS); an **end cluster** is a range where ends
pile up, found from the pooled ends of all six replicates without reading the stage; **other** holds the ends in no
cluster; a **supported** gene or cluster passes stageR at a gene-level overall false discovery rate (OFDR) of 5
percent in the primary analysis; an **event** is one supported cluster with its interpretation.

## Conclusion

**Among 742 adequately measured genes, 52 show strong evidence of a different 3' end between bloodstream and procyclic forms**, based on replicate-aware changes in the end proportions of the reads that carry the gene (reads covering its CDS) and predefined criteria for effect size (30 points of usage), reproducibility (replicates separated; same cluster, same direction under two other cluster definitions) and origin (20 or more such reads at the cluster). 52 of them are found again with the intergenic windows. The list is `docs/v2/strong_end_usage_nanopore.tsv`; the figures are in `docs/v2/figures/index_strong.html`. The broader discovery lists below are the candidates this rule was applied to.

In a prespecified family of 2891 genes (nanopore windows) and 3182 genes
(intergenic windows) with at least 10 poly(A) read ends in every replicate, end clusters were defined from the pooled
ends of all replicates and tested for a change in relative usage between bloodstream (BSF) and procyclic (PCF) forms
with DEXSeq and stageR. 230 nanopore-window genes and 352 intergenic-window genes are supported at a nominal
gene-level OFDR of 5 percent, 227 in both. The two window types are separate families of hypotheses; no rate
is claimed for their union.

The 388 supported nanopore clusters are interpreted one by one: 291 gene-connected, 53 unresolved, insufficient evidence, 39 mixed, 5 transcript-associated.
A gene has no class; the labels belong to sites. Transcript-associated means a catalogue transcript, an annotated
non-coding RNA or a trans-splice acceptor cluster downstream of the CDS ends at the site; mixed means that and a share
of reads linked to the gene; unresolved means the reads do not settle it. Informative reads are those whose 5' end
was recovered (spliced leader detected, or start at the annotated transcript start). A sensitivity run that also
counts reproducible alignment starts as informative is reported separately.

Independent transcripts (full-length reads outside any CDS, plus annotated non-coding RNAs) form their own family of
68 records with enough full-length reads; 16 change in relative abundance between
stages (edgeR, BH 5 percent over that family). This is the only test behind any statement that a non-coding RNA changes
abundance. Of the nanopore events with a catalogue record at their end, 50 have one, 5 of those records were testable and 0 change in abundance.

The procedure was frozen before this run (docs/PLAN_v2.md). The 5 percent is the nominal OFDR of that procedure under its
assumptions (the six libraries are exchangeable apart from stage; DEXSeq's negative-binomial model with trend-shrunk
dispersions). Calibration was not verified: with three replicates per stage no permutation test can do so. The true split gives 230 supported genes. ## Gene-assigned end usage and the count of genes with strong evidence (Part 7)

The window test compares a cluster with the rest of its window, and a window can hold ends of other transcripts.
The gene-assigned analysis counts only reads assigned to the gene: at least 50 aligned bases inside the gene's CDS,
mapping quality 20 or more, no secondary alignment scoring at least as high as the primary, and no other CDS covered
on the same strand. Such a read carries the gene's coding sequence at a placement no other placement matches, so its
end is taken as an end of the gene's transcript, as far as the alignments can tell; reads of uncertain origin are set
aside with their reason. Same windows, same clusters, same model. A gene is flagged **strong end usage** when at least one supported cluster changes by 30 or more points of
gene-assigned usage with the replicates separated, the same cluster changes in the same direction under both other
cluster settings, and the cluster holds 20 or more assigned ends (docs/PLAN_v2.md, Part 7). The flag is a fixed
evidence filter on top of the stageR result; it carries no error rate of its own. Ends inside the CDS count as
alternative 3' ends, not as alternative 3' UTRs.
